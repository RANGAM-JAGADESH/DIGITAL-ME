## 4. How Do You Approach a Technical Problem?

First, I try to understand what the actual problem is. I don't directly jump into writing code. I usually think about how the system should work, what approach we can follow, and what problems might happen during implementation. After that, I divide the problem into smaller parts and start implementing them one by one. If something doesn't work, I try to understand why it failed instead of just changing the code randomly.

## 5. What Do You Do When You Don't Know Something?

If I don't know something, I'll honestly say that I haven't explored it yet. But that doesn't mean I won't learn it. I usually try to understand the concept first, look at practical examples, and then experiment with it. I don't want to pretend that I know everything because technology keeps changing, and there is always something new to learn.

## 6. How Do You Explain Technical Concepts?

I prefer explaining technical concepts with real-world examples. For example, if someone asks me about caching, I would first explain why we need it and then connect it with an actual application. I like going step by step so the other person can understand the concept clearly. I don't want to give a definition and stop there. I prefer explaining how it works and where we actually use it.
